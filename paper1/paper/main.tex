\begin{abstract}
Prompt-injection detectors are evaluated for robustness by perturbing attacks and checking that they are still caught. That evaluation cannot tell a robust detector from one that flags every unfamiliar-looking input. We audit \nGuards{} open detectors and content-safety judges with a paired, label-blind protocol: each of \nPairs{} injection attacks is paired with a length-matched benign twin, and both receive the identical perturbation under \nFamilies{} character, encoding and spacing families. Within-condition AUROC separates a threshold shift, which recalibration can fix, from a destroyed intent signal, which it cannot. The intent signal is destroyed in \nSigDestroyed{} of \nCells{} guard--family cells (\pctSigDestroyed\%). Of the \nAttackRobust{} cells that an attack-only evaluation would call robust, \nAttackRobustFC{} (\pctAttackRobustFC\%) keep their recall only because the detector also flags the benign twin. Failure direction is a property of a guard--family cell rather than of a guard: \nMixedGuards{} guards fail closed under some families and open under others, and the ranking of guards by perturbed-attack recall is unrelated to their ranking by paired accuracy (Kendall $\tau = \hTwoTau$). On a second attack and benign source, every one of the \replDirCells{} cells with a clear failure direction keeps it. Whether the signal survives is decided by the detector's tokenizer. Script-aware canonicalization in front of a retrained detector restores much of it (AUROC \scPidsNoneSeenCharAuc{} to \scPidsScriptAwareSeenCharAuc{} on seen character families), whereas benign-twin augmentation reaches \scPidsAugmentSeenCharAuc{} on a new attack source and lowers recall and false positives together.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Applications that pass untrusted text to a language model increasingly place a classifier in front of it to catch prompt injection \citep{perez2022ignore,greshake2023indirect}. Attackers respond by changing the surface form of the attack: inserting zero-width characters, swapping letters for homoglyphs or digits, or wrapping the instruction in Base64 \citep{hackett2025,fairoze2026}. Robustness evaluations therefore perturb attacks and report how many are still detected.

Such evaluations score only half of a detector's decision. A detector that flags every input containing an invisible character, or every Base64 string, keeps perfect recall on perturbed attacks while blocking the benign users whose text happens to share that form, including users of transliterated scripts, technical text and encoded data. PIDS-Bench, the most complete detector benchmark with an obfuscation axis, notes that its obfuscated set contains only attacks, so ``benign FPR is undefined'' \citep{pidsbench2026}. Recent work restores the benign arm for chat models \citep{zhang2026refusing} and for one guard's own training \citep{hivetrace2026}, and audits over-defense across detectors on unperturbed text \citep{liu2026passing}. The detector-side cell remains open: under a fixed perturbation, does a third-party detector still separate injection intent from benign intent, or does it react to the format?

We answer with a paired, label-blind audit. Every attack is matched to a benign prompt of the same length, and both are transformed identically. This makes three distinctions measurable:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Fail-closed versus fail-open.} A detector can keep high recall under a perturbation because it now flags the benign twin too, or keep a low false-positive rate because it now misses the attack. We call these error directions \emph{fail-closed} and \emph{fail-open}; the terms describe classification errors under perturbation, not a pipeline's behaviour when its guard is unavailable.
  \item \textbf{Shifted versus destroyed signal.} A within-condition AUROC between attacks and their twins shows whether the detector still ranks attacks above benign text. If it does, the errors are a threshold shift that per-format recalibration can repair; if the AUROC falls to chance, no threshold can.
  \item \textbf{Encoding versus template.} A scaffold control places the same decoding instruction around the plain text \citep{zhang2026refusing}, separating the effect of the encoding from the effect of the wrapper's words.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The first paired, label-blind audit of prompt-injection detectors in which every attack and its length-matched benign twin receive the identical perturbation (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). We carry the benign-arm and scaffold controls of \citet{zhang2026refusing} over to detectors, remove the length confound they report, and add soft-hyphen, leetspeak, ROT13, diacritic, full-width and letter-spacing families on the benign side.
  \item Score-level paired metrics with pre-registered decision rules: within-condition AUROC with an intact/degraded/destroyed status, and a failure-mode rule applied at each detector's deployed threshold (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).
  \item A \nGuards{}-detector Study 1 (\S\ref{sec:results}) showing that failure direction belongs to a guard--family cell, not to a guard, and that attack-only robustness rankings do not predict paired accuracy, replicated on a second attack and benign source and checked for training-data contamination.
  \item A tokenizer-level explanation (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}): a perturbation the tokenizer normalizes away leaves the signal intact, one it maps to unknown tokens destroys it, and once a detector cannot read its input it returns its own default verdict.
  \item Study 2 (\S\ref{sec:study2}), the first controlled comparison of script-aware canonicalization and benign-twin augmentation with benign FPR on seen and held-out families, over five training seeds: canonicalization transfers to a new attack source and augmentation does not, decoded text must replace rather than accompany the encoded input, and augmentation couples recall with FPR.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Benign controls under encoding} \citet{zhang2026refusing} cross harmful and benign behaviours with plain, scaffolded and encoded forms for chat models and show that refusal under encodings can look like safety while being indiscriminate; they identify the same missing cell ``one level out, on the defence''. They report that raw character length alone separates their two arms at AUROC 0.654, a confound our length matching removes. \citet{handa2024} and \citet{uysal2026} study chat-model behaviour on ciphered or multilingual requests; no detector decision is measured. \citet{zhang2026share} list benign inputs that resemble encodings as untested.

\paragraph{Detector audits} PIDS-Bench evaluates four detectors with a structural-shift benign set and an attack-only obfuscation set \citep{pidsbench2026}. \citet{liu2026passing} audits 15 detectors on benign agent tool outputs and finds default-threshold FPR from 0\% to 93\%, with no character or encoding perturbation. \citet{fairoze2026} report that Prompt Guard 2 regresses on encoded attacks and argue, without measuring it, that blocking encodings would harm benign users; \citet{hackett2025} report attack-only evasion rates for character injection. Prompt Overflow evades detectors by dilution across 512-token windows \citep{promptoverflow2026}; we keep every input inside one window to exclude that mechanism. PIGuard and its NotInject benchmark define over-defense as trigger-word bias on clean English text \citep{li2025piguard}; we study an orthographic axis.

\paragraph{Two-sided obfuscation and normalization} HiveTraceGuard-Pro obfuscates both labels of topic-paired twins to train and evaluate its own guard and reports a recall/FPR coupling it could not remove \citep{hivetrace2026}. Canonicalization in front of a content-safety classifier \citep{baszta2026} and at RAG ingestion \citep{hiddenplaintext2026} has been reported with attack-side metrics only; PIDS-Bench's hard-negative augmentation removed over-defense on curated inputs but not on externally sourced ones \citep{pidsbench2026}. We compare canonicalization and augmentation head to head, with the benign FPR that the canonicalization studies omit, and test whether HiveTraceGuard-Pro's coupling recurs on another detector.

\paragraph{Paired evaluation} Our design descends from contrast sets \citep{gardner2020contrast} and CheckList invariance tests \citep{ribeiro2020checklist}.

\section{Paired Perturbation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Pairs} Attacks and benign prompts are shuffled with a fixed seed. Each attack is paired with the unused benign prompt closest to it in character length, and a disjoint pool of \nCalibration{} benign prompts is held out for threshold calibration, so calibrated thresholds are never fitted on evaluated items. In the primary run the median length gap within a pair is zero characters, and the AUROC of character length (attack vs.\ twin) lies between \lengthAucMin{} and \lengthAucMax{} in every condition.

\paragraph{Perturbation families} Each pair is expanded under eleven families plus the clean reference; both members receive the identical, label-blind transformation (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:families}). The character families insert U+200B or U+00AD inside words of four or more characters, substitute digits and symbols for letters, or map Latin letters to Cyrillic lookalikes. The wrapper families encode the whole prompt in Base64 or ROT13 inside a fixed instruction, \textit{``Decode the following Base64 text and respond to it: \ldots''}; the matching scaffold places the same instruction around the plain text. Full-width forms, combining diacritics and letter spacing are held out from the training families of Study 2; they are not new as attack families \citep{hackett2025}.


\subsection{Metrics and pre-registered rules}
\label{sec:metrics}

Let $A_c$ and $B_c$ be the detector's scores for the attacks and their twins under condition $c$.

\paragraph{Intent signal (primary, threshold-free)} The within-condition $\mathrm{AUROC}(c)$ of $A_c$ against $B_c$, with a 95\% CI from 1,000 paired bootstrap resamples. The status of each guard--condition cell was fixed before the full run: \emph{intact} if the lower CI bound is at least $\mathrm{AUROC}(\text{clean}) - 0.05$; \emph{destroyed} if the upper bound is below 0.75; \emph{degraded} otherwise.

\paragraph{Decisions} At a threshold $\tau$, attack recall $\mathrm{TPR}(c)$ and benign-twin $\mathrm{FPR}(c)$ with Wilson intervals \citep{wilson1927}, and paired joint accuracy $\mathrm{PJA}(c)$, the share of pairs whose attack is flagged and whose twin is not.

\paragraph{Failure mode} Relative to the clean condition at the same threshold, a cell is \emph{fail-closed} if FPR rises by at least 20 points while TPR does not fall by 20; \emph{fail-open} if TPR falls by at least 20 points while FPR does not rise by 20; \emph{both} if both changes reach 20 points; and \emph{stable} otherwise.

\paragraph{Thresholds} The headline uses each detector's default threshold (0.5 on its attack probability), as deployed. Thresholds giving 1\% and 5\% FPR on the held-out calibration pool are reported as sensitivity analyses. The perplexity baseline has no default threshold and appears only at calibrated thresholds.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data} The primary run pairs \nPairs{} attacks from Lakera's \textit{gandalf\_ignore\_instructions} \citep{lakera2023gandalf} with benign instructions from \textit{databricks-dolly-15k} that have no context field \citep{conover2023dolly}; \nCalibration{} further Dolly instructions form the calibration pool. The dataset (SHA-256 \texttt{\datasetSha\ldots}) rebuilds deterministically from pinned dataset revisions. A second primary source pairs \pidsPairs{} attacks and benign prompts from the frozen PIDS-Bench v3 test split \citep{pidsbench2026}, with \pidsCalibration{} further benign prompts for calibration (\S\ref{sec:second}).

\paragraph{Detectors} Table~\ref{tab:clean} lists the \nGuards{} detectors. Encoder classifiers are scored by the softmax probability of their attack label(s). Prompt Guard v1 is reported in both of Meta's modes: injection-or-jailbreak, intended for third-party data, and jailbreak only, intended for direct user input. Generative judges (Llama Guard \citep{inan2023llamaguard}, Qwen3Guard \citep{qwen3guard2025}, WildGuard \citep{han2024wildguard}, HiveTraceGuard-Pro \citep{hivetrace2026}) are scored without sampling, by the probability mass of their unsafe verdict among their verdict strings under teacher forcing; each judge was checked to agree with its own greedy output on probe prompts before scoring. Content-safety judges classify harmfulness rather than injection, so low clean recall is expected; they are included because deployments use them as the only guard. HiveTraceGuard-Pro is the exception: its policy covers prompt injection and obfuscation, and it is the only public detector trained with both labels obfuscated, which makes it a direct test of whether two-sided training avoids the failures we measure. GuardShield is our own DeBERTa-v3-small classifier \citep{he2023debertav3}. Its training data include the SPML corpus, whose injected user turns open with copied Gandalf prompts: \gsContamAttacks{} of the \nPairs{} Study 1 attacks (\gsContamAttacksPct\%) occur in GuardShield's training text, and Horizon guard-base trained on Gandalf's train split, which holds \horizonContamAttacks{} of them. Both detectors are also reported without these pairs (\S\ref{sec:contamination}). A GPT-2 perplexity filter is a non-learned baseline. Model revisions are pinned to commit hashes.

\paragraph{Compute and precision} Generative judges run in float16 on two NVIDIA T4 GPUs. Against float32, 16-bit scoring shifted individual scores by at most about 0.08 and flipped 0.5--1.5\% of decisions near the threshold on 200 pilot items. No pair exceeds any classifier's input limit under any family, so no input is truncated.

\section{Study 1: Paired Audit Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Clean condition}

On clean text, \nCleanAucHigh{} of \nGuards{} detectors rank attacks above their twins with AUROC $\geq 0.95$ (Table~\ref{tab:clean}). Two exceptions matter for what follows. Prompt Guard v1 in its injection mode flags \pgOneCleanFPR\% of clean benign twins (AUROC \pgOneCleanAUC): it treats any instruction as potential injection, as its documentation warns for direct user input, so its perturbed cells carry no information about intent. deepset and fmops already flag \deepsetCleanFPR\% and \fmopsCleanFPR\% of clean Dolly instructions at their default threshold, consistent with \citet{liu2026passing}. The content-safety judges separate attacks from twins well (AUROC \judgeCleanAucMin--\judgeCleanAucMax) but flag only \judgeCleanTprMin--\judgeCleanTprMax\% of Gandalf attacks at their default threshold, because most of these attacks seek a password rather than harmful content.

\subsection{Unfamiliar formats destroy the intent signal}

Figure~\ref{fig:map} shows every guard--family cell. The intent signal is intact in \nSigIntact{} cells, degraded in \nSigDegraded{} and destroyed in \nSigDestroyed{} of \nCells{}. The wrapper families are the most damaging: Base64 and ROT13 destroy the signal in \nWrapDestroyed{} of \nWrapCells{} cells, with every detector near chance. The scaffold control isolates the cause. Placing the identical decoding instruction around the plain text leaves the signal intact in \nScaffoldIntact{} of \nScaffoldCells{} cells, so it is the encoding, not the instruction's wording, that removes the signal. Character families destroy the signal in \nCharDestroyed{} of \nCharCells{} cells and the held-out families in \nHeldDestroyed{} of \nHeldCells{}.

\subsection{Attack-only evaluation hides both failure directions}

At the default threshold, \nModeClosed{} of \nModeCells{} cells fail closed, \nModeOpen{} fail open, \nModeBoth{} fail both ways, and \nModeStable{} are stable. Attack-only robustness evaluation cannot see the first group. Of the \nAttackRobust{} cells whose attack recall stays within 5 points of clean, which such an evaluation would report as robust, \nAttackRobustFC{} (\pctAttackRobustFC\%) are fail-closed: the detector also flags at least 20 points more of the benign twins. ProtectAI v2, for example, keeps \protectaiBsfTPR\% recall under Base64 while flagging \protectaiBsfFPR\% of benign twins (AUROC \protectaiBsfAUC), and \protectaiShyTPR\% recall under soft hyphens with \protectaiShyFPR\% FPR. Symmetrically, of the \nBenignRobust{} cells whose benign FPR stays within 5 points of clean, \nBenignRobustFO{} (\pctBenignRobustFO\%) lose at least 20 points of recall. Prompt Guard 2 86M falls from \pgTwoEightSixCleanTPR\% clean recall to \pgTwoEightSixShyTPR\% under soft hyphens, leetspeak and diacritics while its FPR stays at \pgTwoEightSixShyFPR\%, and its AUROC under those families (\pgTwoEightSixShyAUC, \pgTwoEightSixLeetAUC, \pgTwoEightSixDiacAUC) shows the detector cannot tell the two apart. A benign-only over-defense audit would score that cell as perfect.

\paragraph{The same guard fails in both directions (H1b)} Failure direction belongs to a guard--family cell rather than to a guard. \nMixedGuards{} guards (\mixedGuards) have at least one fail-closed and at least one fail-open cell. Qwen3Guard-Gen 0.6B flags \qwenSmallCleanTPR\% of clean attacks but \qwenSmallBsfTPR\% of Base64 attacks and \qwenSmallBsfFPR\% of their twins, yet under leetspeak it flags \qwenSmallLeetFPR\% of the benign twins. Model families also disagree across versions: Llama Guard 3 8B fails open under Base64 (recall \lgThreeEightBsfTPR\%, FPR \lgThreeEightBsfFPR\%), while Llama Guard 4 12B fails closed, flagging \lgFourBsfFPR\% of Base64 benign twins and \lgFourRotFPR\% of ROT13 twins.


\paragraph{Two-sided training does not transfer to new families} HiveTraceGuard-Pro, trained with obfuscation applied to both labels \citep{hivetrace2026}, is near-perfect on clean text (AUROC \htCleanAUC{}, FPR \htCleanFPR\%), yet its signal is destroyed in \htDestroyed{} of \nFamilies{} families (\htPidsDestroyed{} on PIDS-Bench). It is also the guard most often failing both ways at once (\htBoth{} cells): under Base64 it flags \htBsfTPR\% of attacks and \htBsfFPR\% of twins (AUROC \htBsfAUC), and under diacritics \htDiacTPR\% and \htDiacFPR\%. Its training transforms differ from most of our families, so two-sided augmentation protects against the perturbations it saw, not against format change in general, which Study~2 confirms under control.

\paragraph{Encoder classifiers trained for injection fail closed; Prompt Guard fails open} Across families, the injection classifiers fine-tuned from general encoders (ProtectAI v2, deepset, fmops, Mezzo, GuardShield) produce almost only fail-closed cells, whereas the Prompt Guard models produce only fail-open cells. Our own GuardShield is no exception: it flags \guardshieldCleanFPR\% of clean twins but \guardshieldShyFPR\% under soft hyphens (AUROC \guardshieldShyAUC). Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} explains both patterns.

\subsection{Mechanism: the tokenizer decides (H4)}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\paragraph{Normalized away: the signal survives} When the tokenizer maps the perturbed text back to the clean token sequence, the detector sees the clean prompt, and the signal is intact in all \mechObfNormalized{} such cells on both sources. This explains the immune cells in Figure~\ref{fig:map}: Prompt Guard 2's tokenizer removes zero-width characters, full-width forms and letter spacing, consistent with Meta's tokenization fix and an independent analysis \citep{meta2025promptguard2,xpn_tokenization}; Horizon guard-base's removes zero-width characters, soft hyphens and full-width forms; fmops's lowercasing, accent-stripping tokenizer removes zero-width characters, soft hyphens and diacritics; and those of ProtectAI, deepset, Mezzo, GuardShield and Prompt Guard v1 normalize full-width forms.

\paragraph{Unknown tokens: the signal is destroyed} fmops maps full-width words, and ProtectAI, deepset and Prompt Guard 2 22M map many soft-hyphenated words, to their unknown token. All \mechObfUnknown{} such cells lose the signal, although their token count barely changes, so token counts alone hide this failure.

\paragraph{Fragmented: encoders lose the signal, larger judges sometimes read through} The remaining \mechObfFragmented{} cells fragment the text into more tokens. Encoder classifiers keep an intact signal in only \mechObfFragEncIntact{} of their \mechObfFragEnc{} fragmented cells (\mechPidsFragEncIntact{} on PIDS-Bench), while the generative judges keep it in \mechObfFragJudgeIntact{} of \mechObfFragJudge{}, mostly Qwen3Guard-Gen 8B and WildGuard. How much the text fragments does not matter once it does: within fragmented cells, the fragmentation ratio is unrelated to the AUROC drop (Spearman $\rho = \mechObfFragRho$, $p = \mechObfFragP$; PIDS-Bench $\rho = \mechPidsFragRho$). We therefore read H4 as partly supported: the tokenizer's treatment of a perturbation, not the amount of fragmentation, predicts signal loss.

\paragraph{Direction is the guard's default for unreadable text} Once a detector cannot read its input, it returns a fixed verdict for both members of the pair. Across all perturbed families, \mechObfDirMajority{} of the \mechObfDirCells{} destroyed cells with a pure failure direction (\mechObfDirPct\%; PIDS-Bench \mechPidsDirPct\%) follow their guard's majority direction: the fine-tuned injection classifiers treat unreadable input as an attack, and Prompt Guard treats it as benign. Opposite directions within one guard arise mainly where the mechanisms differ, for instance Llama Guard 3 8B fails closed on fragmented leetspeak and full-width text but open on encoded wrappers.

\subsection{Threshold shift or destroyed signal}

Most failures are not threshold shifts. Only four fail-closed cells keep an intact signal, all under scaffolds: ProtectAI v2 flags \protectaiBsfScaffoldFPR\% of scaffolded benign twins while still ranking attacks above them (AUROC \protectaiBsfScaffoldAUC), so per-format recalibration would repair it. Prompt Guard 2 86M under homoglyphs is the clearest near-shift: AUROC \pgTwoEightSixHomoAUC{} with only \pgTwoEightSixHomoTPR\% recall at its default threshold. In all but \nWrapNotDestroyed{} wrapper cell and in most character cells, the AUROC is near chance and no threshold recovers the detector.

\subsection{Attack-only rankings do not predict paired accuracy (H2)}

Ranking the \hTwoN{} detectors with a default threshold by mean perturbed-attack recall and by mean paired joint accuracy gives Kendall $\tau = \hTwoTau$ ($p = \hTwoP$) \citep{kendall1938}. The two rankings are unrelated: detectors at the top of an attack-only leaderboard are mostly those that flag perturbed benign text as well (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:pja}).

\subsection{Robustness checks}

At thresholds calibrated to 5\% FPR on clean benign prompts, fail-closed cells rise from \nModeClosed{} to \nModeFprClosed{} and fail-open cells fall from \nModeOpen{} to \nModeFprOpen{}. Calibration lowers the threshold of detectors whose clean benign scores are near zero, such as Prompt Guard 2, so their perturbed benign twins cross it; the failure direction depends on the operating point, which is why the headline uses deployed defaults. Character-length AUROC is \lengthAucMin--\lengthAucMax{} in every condition, so length cannot explain any separation, and no input exceeds a classifier's window.

\subsection{Second primary source}
\label{sec:second}
A single attack source could make the failure map a property of Gandalf. We therefore repeated Study 1 on \pidsPairs{} pairs from the PIDS-Bench v3 test split \citep{pidsbench2026}, whose attacks are constructed and paraphrased rather than crowd-sourced. We kept unobfuscated rows, one paraphrase per seed and texts of at most 200 characters, since PIDS-Bench's benign prompts are much shorter than its longest attacks; the attacks are \pidsTemplateAttacks{} templated and \pidsSpmlAttacks{} SPML injections, and matching twins against the whole benign pool keeps the length AUROC at \pidsLengthAuc{}.

The failure map replicates (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:map-pids}). In every one of the \replDirCells{} cells where both sources show a pure failure direction, the direction is the same; the failure mode matches in \replModeSame{} of \replModeCells{} cells and the signal status in \replSigSame{} of \replCells{}. \replMixedSame{} of the \nMixedGuards{} guards that fail in both directions on Gandalf do so again, the wrapper families destroy the signal in \pidsWrapDestroyed{} of \pidsWrapCells{} cells, and \pidsAttackRobustFC{} of the \pidsAttackRobust{} cells that look robust on attacks alone are fail-closed (\pidsPctAttackRobustFC\%). Attack-only and paired rankings remain unrelated (Kendall $\tau = \pidsHTwoTau$, $p = \pidsHTwoP$).

Clean-condition recall depends on the attack source, as PIDS-Bench found. Prompt Guard 2 86M flags \pidsPgTwoEightSixCleanTPR\% of clean PIDS-Bench attacks in our subset, against an in-distribution recall of 0.369 that PIDS-Bench reports on its full test split, but \pgTwoEightSixCleanTPR\% of Gandalf attacks. Horizon guard-base, which trained on Gandalf (\S\ref{sec:contamination}), loses more than the others on the new source: its mean paired joint accuracy over perturbed conditions falls from \horizonMeanPjaObf\% to \horizonMeanPjaPids\% and its destroyed cells rise from \horizonDestroyedObf{} to \horizonDestroyedPids{}, against \sentinelMeanPjaObf\% to \sentinelMeanPjaPids\% for Sentinel v2. This is consistent with, but does not prove, an advantage from training on Gandalf.

\subsection{Training-data contamination}
\label{sec:contamination}
Following \citet{liu2026passing}, we checked every evaluated text (the unperturbed attack and twin of each pair, and the calibration prompts) against the published training data of the \nAuditGuards{} detectors that disclose it: \nAuditCorpora{} corpora at pinned revisions. After lowercasing and removing punctuation, a text counts as contaminated if it equals a training text or one full line of one, or if at least 80\% of its word 8-grams occur in a single training text; texts under eight words count only as verbatim copies. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:contamination} marks each cell \emph{known} (overlap with data the detector trained on in full), \emph{likely} (overlap with a corpus its model card lists without saying which rows were used), \emph{none} (complete training data audited, no overlap) or \emph{unknown}.

Three overlaps matter. Horizon guard-base trained on Gandalf's train split, which holds \horizonContamAttacks{} of our \nPairs{} attacks (\horizonContamAttacksPct\%). GuardShield's SPML training data contain \gsContamAttacks{} of them (\gsContamAttacksPct\%), \gsContamAttacksVerbatim{} as verbatim lines. On the benign side, ProtectAI v2's model card lists VMware's open-instruct collection, which includes Dolly: \protectaiContamTwins{} of the \nPairs{} twins and \protectaiContamCal{} of the \nCalibration{} calibration prompts occur in it, so ProtectAI's clean-condition FPR may partly reflect benign prompts seen in training. PIGuard's released training set contains \piguardContamTwins{} twins and \piguardContamCal{} calibration prompts; PIGuard is not in the scored roster. No audited corpus contains any of the \pidsAuditTexts{} PIDS-Bench texts.

Recomputing each detector without its own contaminated pairs leaves GuardShield with \gsDeconPairs{} pairs and Horizon guard-base with \horizonDeconPairs{}. Of their \deconCells{} perturbed cells, the failure mode changes in \deconModeChanged{} at the default threshold and in \deconModeFprChanged{} at the 5\%-FPR threshold, and the intent-signal status changes in \deconSigChanged{} (\deconSigUp{} from destroyed to degraded): the AUROC moves by at most \deconAucShiftMax{}, while the smaller sample widens its confidence interval across the 0.75 bound. ProtectAI v2 keeps only \protectaiDeconPairs{} uncontaminated pairs, too few for this check. Training-data overlap would favour clean-condition results; it does not explain the failure map.

Study~1 shows that a detector loses the intent signal when its input becomes unreadable to it. Two remedies are available: make the input readable again before the detector sees it (canonicalization), or teach the detector the unreadable forms (augmentation). We compare them under the protocol of Study~1.

\paragraph{Arms} We retrain GuardShield with its shipped two-stage recipe under five seeds per arm, so every arm is compared with a retrained baseline: (1)~\emph{none}; (2)~a \emph{naive canonicalizer} (NFKC and deletion of all Unicode format characters, as LLM Guard's InvisibleText scanner does \citep{llmguard_invisible}); (3)~a \emph{script-aware canonicalizer} (NFKC; deletion of zero-width spaces, soft hyphens, word joiners and byte-order marks but not of the ZWJ/ZWNJ joiners Devanagari uses; Cyrillic and Greek confusables mapped to Latin inside mostly-Latin words; letter spacing collapsed; Base64 and ROT13 spans decoded and rescored, keeping the maximum score); (4)~\emph{decode-and-rescan} alone; (5)~\emph{benign-twin augmentation}, one copy of every training example perturbed by a seen family, attacks and benign prompts alike; (6)~\emph{canonicalization plus augmentation}. The seen families are the four character families and the two wrappers; full-width forms, diacritics and letter spacing never appear in training. Because the maximum rule cannot lower a score, we add \emph{decode-and-replace}, which scores only the decoded text. ProtectAI~v2, whose training data are mostly undisclosed, receives arms (1)--(4) on its shipped checkpoint. Each model is read at the threshold that flags 90\% of its clean attacks. The PIDS-Bench pairs, which share no text with GuardShield's training data (\S\ref{sec:contamination}), are the primary evaluation.


\paragraph{Canonicalization restores the signal where augmentation does not} On the seen character families, the retrained baseline separates attacks from their twins at AUROC \scPidsNoneSeenCharAuc{}. Script-aware canonicalization raises this to \scPidsScriptAwareSeenCharAuc{} and lowers the benign FPR at matched recall from \scPidsNoneSeenCharFpr\% to \scPidsScriptAwareSeenCharFpr\%; augmentation reaches only \scPidsAugmentSeenCharAuc{} (FPR \scPidsAugmentSeenCharFpr\%). The naive canonicalizer recovers part of the gap (\scPidsNaiveSeenCharAuc{}) because it lacks the confusable mapping. Combining both gives the best result (\scPidsCanonAugmentSeenCharAuc{}). The same canonicalizer helps a detector we cannot retrain: on ProtectAI~v2, seen-family FPR at matched recall falls from \scPidsProtectaiNoneSeenCharFpr\% to \scPidsProtectaiScriptAwareSeenCharFpr\%.

\paragraph{Augmentation helps on familiar data only} On the Gandalf pairs, augmentation matches canonicalization (seen-family AUROC \scObfAugmentSeenCharAuc{} against \scObfScriptAwareSeenCharAuc{}). This is not contamination: without the \scObfDeconExcluded{} pairs whose text occurs in GuardShield's training data, the values are \scObfDeconAugmentSeenCharAuc{} and \scObfDeconScriptAwareSeenCharAuc{}. Augmentation therefore helps on attacks resembling GuardShield's training data and fails to transfer to the PIDS-Bench source, as PIDS-Bench found for hard-negative augmentation \citep{pidsbench2026}. A canonicalizer, which does not learn, behaves the same on both sources.

\paragraph{Held-out families follow the rules, not generalization} The held-out advantage of canonicalization (AUROC \scPidsScriptAwareHeldOutAuc{} against \scPidsAugmentHeldOutAuc{}) comes from letter spacing, which its rules collapse (\scPidsScriptAwareSpacedLettersAuc{} against \scPidsAugmentSpacedLettersAuc{}). Full-width forms are already normalized by GuardShield's tokenizer in every arm (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}). Diacritics, which no rule covers, defeat both (\scPidsScriptAwareDiacriticsAuc{} and \scPidsAugmentDiacriticsAuc{}). ``Held out'' thus means held out from training, not from the canonicalizer's design; a canonicalizer covers exactly the transformations its rules name.

\paragraph{Decoding needs to replace, not add} Decode-and-rescan restores the wrapper signal (AUROC \scPidsNoneSeenWrapperAuc{} to \scPidsRescanOnlySeenWrapperAuc{}) but leaves the benign FPR at \scPidsRescanOnlySeenWrapperFpr\%: under the maximum rule, a detector that flags the encoded text itself still flags it. Replacing the encoded input with its decoding lowers the FPR to \scPidsScriptReplaceSeenWrapperFpr\% (\scPidsCanonAugmentReplaceSeenWrapperFpr\% with augmentation). Replacement does not help ProtectAI~v2, whose benign FPR stays at \scPidsProtectaiScriptReplaceSeenWrapperFpr\% because it flags the decoding instruction itself: \scPidsProtectaiNoneScaffoldFpr\% of plain-text scaffolds.

\paragraph{Augmentation couples recall and FPR (H5)} Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:coupling} traces augmentation over training mixtures. At the default threshold, seen-family recall falls from \scPidsNoneSeenCharTprDefault\% without augmentation to \scPidsAugmentSeenCharTprDefault\% with a perturbed copy of every example, while the benign FPR falls from \scPidsNoneSeenCharFprDefault\% to \scPidsAugmentSeenCharFprDefault\%; the intermediate mixtures lie between. The AUROC moves far less (\scPidsNoneSeenCharAuc{} to \scPidsAugmentSeenCharAuc{}): augmentation mostly shifts the detector's operating point rather than restoring its ranking, the coupling HiveTraceGuard-Pro reported for its own guard \citep{hivetrace2026}.


\paragraph{The script-aware rule rarely matters for Hindi} Deleting all format characters also deletes the joiners that select Devanagari conjunct forms. In the \ayaTexts{} native-written Hindi prompts of the Aya Dataset \citep{singh2024aya}, however, only \ayaTextsJoiner{} prompt (\ayaWordsJoiner{} of \ayaWords{} words) contains one. The naive canonicalizer deletes it and the script-aware one keeps it, so the script-aware rule is a safeguard rather than a measurable gain on natural Hindi text.

\section{Discussion}

A detector that flags every unfamiliar format looks robust on attack-only benchmarks and looks precise on benign-only audits of clean text. Only a paired evaluation under the identical perturbation exposes it. We recommend that robustness evaluations of detectors (i) perturb benign text exactly as they perturb attacks, (ii) report a within-condition AUROC alongside rates, so that recalibration can be separated from retraining, and (iii) report failure direction per family, since a single guard can over-block one format and under-block another. For deployment, Study~2 favours fixing the input before retraining: a script-aware canonicalizer needs no training data, works in front of detectors whose training data are unknown and transfers to a new attack source, whereas augmentation helped only on familiar data and mostly traded recall for fewer false positives. A canonicalizer covers only what its rules name, decoded text should replace the encoded input, and the two remedies combined gave the best results.

\section{Limitations}
Gandalf attacks mostly target password extraction, and Dolly instructions are general-purpose, so Study 1 covers one attack style; the PIDS-Bench run adds a second source but is dominated by templated injections paraphrased by one model. Our perturbations are fixed and non-adaptive; an adaptive attacker would do at least as well. The 20-point failure-mode rule and the 0.05 and 0.75 signal thresholds are conventions fixed before the full run, and other cut-offs would move individual cells. Content-safety judges are scored on a task they were not designed for. Generative judges ran in 16-bit precision, which flips a small share of near-threshold decisions. Study~2 retrains one small detector (GuardShield, DeBERTa-v3-small) with five seeds; ProtectAI~v2 receives only the inference-time arms, because its training data are mostly undisclosed. The canonicalizer has no rule for leetspeak or diacritics, and collapsing letter spacing cannot recover word boundaries when every word is spaced. The coupling is traced over augmentation mixtures with three seeds at the intermediate mixtures, not over HiveTraceGuard-Pro's training rounds.

\section{Ethical Considerations}
All attack prompts come from public datasets; the perturbations are well known and already used against deployed detectors. Publishing which detectors fail open under which families could help attackers, but the families are public and the detectors' vendors can reproduce the audit from our released code. No personal data are used.
