# Fresh 2025-2026 search: format-triggered over-defense of prompt-injection detectors, and normalization/canonicalization defenses

Search date: 2026-10-13. About 20 search/fetch calls (WebSearch, WebFetch on arXiv HTML). "Fetched" means the arXiv HTML/abs page was read through the fetch tool (long pages were truncated at about 100k characters, so later sections such as appendices may not have been read). "Snippet-only" means the claim comes from a search-result summary and the full text was not checked.

Planned-paper claims being checked: (1) paired benign-twin perturbation protocol (identical label-blind perturbation on each attack and a length-matched benign twin); (2) paired metrics separating "shifted" from "destroyed" signal (paired joint accuracy, within-condition AUROC, FPR at reference TPR); (3) cross-guard fail-closed vs fail-open audit (e.g., Prompt Guard 2 at 0% FPR but near-0% recall under soft hyphen / Base64); (5) script-aware Unicode canonicalization (NFKC, strip ZW/soft hyphen but keep Devanagari ZWJ/ZWNJ, confusables, decode-and-rescan) vs benign-twin augmentation on seen vs held-out families.

## Q1. Any 2026 paper using "benign twin", "paired perturbation", "matched benign", "contrast set" or "minimal pair" evaluation for guardrails or injection detectors?

### Takeaway
Yes, the paired-benign idea is now in print in two September 2026 preprints. Neither one evaluates prompt-injection detectors with identical, length-matched, label-blind perturbations and paired detector metrics. The closest, "Refusing Everything Looks Safe" (2609.26176, Sep 2026), applies identical invertible encodings to harmful and theme-matched benign prompts, but it tests only refusal by 7-8B chat LLMs. HiveTraceGuard-Pro (2609.01046, Sep 2026) builds same-domain safe/harm twins crossed with 8 obfuscation transforms for a guard model, but it is a model paper, and the twins are not described as length-matched. Claim (1) is narrowed but not pre-empted. The novelty has to rest on applying the design to injection detectors, length matching, and the paired metrics.

### Cited Findings
- **"Refusing Everything Looks Safe: Restoring the Benign Arm to Encoded-Prompt Evaluation"** (Zhang, Xu, Luo, et al.; arXiv 2609.26176, cs.CR; v2 dated 24 Sep 2026; v1 date not shown on page). Fetched.
  - The paper argues that encoded-prompt attacks are evaluated almost only on harmful requests. It runs benign requests through the same encodings. Its key line: over-refusal is measured on untransformed prompts and encoded attacks on harmful content, so "benign content in attack form falls to neither" — [arXiv 2609.26176](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26176v2)
  - The design pairs JailbreakBench harmful prompts with "theme-matched" benign prompts (paired by name). Transformations are deterministic, exactly invertible, request-text-only, and the same transform is applied to both arms. The prompts are **not length-matched**: raw length separates the corpora (AUROC 0.654), and length stratification moves the harm gap by at most 0.024 — [arXiv 2609.26176](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26176v2)
  - Encodings tested: homoglyph (Cyrillic/Greek), fullwidth, zero-width (U+200B), Unicode math-bold, base64 and tag-block (unreadable for all models). No ROT13, leetspeak or soft hyphen — [arXiv 2609.26176](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26176v2)
  - Systems tested: only four instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Tulu-3-8B). **No guard classifiers or prompt-injection detectors.** Metrics: refusal rates, harm gap (harmful minus benign refusal), McNemar/Fisher tests. No AUROC within condition, FPR at TPR, or paired joint accuracy — [arXiv 2609.26176](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26176v2)
  - Key numbers: harmful homoglyph refusal spans 0.08 across models vs 0.57 in plaintext; on Llama the harm gap falls from +0.82 (plaintext) to 0.00 under encoding. The paper also lists "twelve instrument defects", most of which "inflate apparent safety." It proposes no normalization defense — [arXiv 2609.26176](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26176v2)
- **HiveTraceGuard-Pro: A Compact Generative Guardrail for Prompt Injection, Jailbreaks, and Adversarial Obfuscation** (Oblakov, Sadiekh, Kokuykin; arXiv 2609.01046v1, 01 Sep 2026; cs.CR). Fetched (HTML, first ~100k characters).
  - The model is a 0.6B generative guard LoRA-tuned from Qwen3-0.6B on Russian and English data. Its robustness set pairs harmful prompts with same-domain benign prompts (e.g., "Fascism" vs "History of fascism") and applies obfuscation to both labels. The text does not say the twins are length-matched or that the same transform instance is applied to both members — [arXiv 2609.01046](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01046v1)
  - Eval transforms: random_case, random_insert_symbols, random_replace_symbols, transliterate_to_latin, wrong_layout, to_lower, to_upper, informal_rewrite. Training transforms: the first five plus zero_width_insert, leet, and phonetic_substitution. **to_lower, to_upper and informal_rewrite are held out (eval-only).** The corpus includes 4,579 paired benign rewrites, so this is benign-twin-style augmentation — [arXiv 2609.01046](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01046v1)
  - Numbers: over-blocking on augmented benign FPR 0.139 vs clean benign FPR 0.016. Harm FNR on augmented 0.123. wrong_layout has the highest FPR (0.262). The held-out informal_rewrite has the highest FNR (0.307). AUROC 0.973 on pooled robustness rows. Baselines are 35 open guards (Qwen3Guard, Llama Guard 3/4, WildGuard, PolyGuard, ...); Prompt Guard 2 and ProtectAI are not named. **No input normalizer** in the shipped path — [arXiv 2609.01046](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01046v1)
  - (Snippet-only) The authors report that gains in obfuscated-harm recall repeatedly raised over-blocking of obfuscated benign requests, or the reverse, and that pooled FPR 0.268 vs FNR 0.156 means over-blocking was more common than missed harm — [arXiv 2609.01046 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01046)
- **Beyond the Verdict: Evidence-Aligned Evaluation of Visual Prompt-Injection Guardrails** (arXiv 2609.05535v1, Sep 2026). Snippet-only. For each divergent (injected) visual instance, it generates an "aligned" benign counterpart rendered on the same page, location and style, so detectors that key on injection-like visual cues fail. This is a matched-benign design in the visual modality — [arXiv 2609.05535](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05535v1)
- An industry-practice analogue: a GitHub PR in the AiSOC project grades prompt-injection detection "against a clean twin". A detection counts only if the injected field fires and the clean twin's same field does not. The authors found earlier scores were inflated without the twin — [beenuar/AiSOC PR #913](https://github.com/beenuar/AiSOC/pull/913) (snippet-only)
- Contrast/minimal-pair search found no injection-detector paper using those terms. The nearest are CAPTURE (arXiv 2505.12368, 2025: context-aware benchmark of attack detection plus over-defense, not matched pairs) and ContrastShield (SSRN 2026, contrastive fine-tuning, not contrast sets) — [CAPTURE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12368); [ContrastShield SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6645520) (both snippet-only)
- Prior over-defense benchmark baseline: InjecGuard/PIGuard NotInject (arXiv 2410.22770; ACL 2025) uses benign sentences with injection trigger words, a lexical rather than format perturbation and not paired — [arXiv 2410.22770](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22770v3)

### Inferences
- 2609.26176 is the most dangerous overlap for claims (1) and (2) at the framing level ("restore the benign arm"; "refusing everything looks safe" is the fail-closed artifact). The planned paper must cite it and set itself apart: it targets injection *detectors* (classifiers with scores), not chat-LLM refusal; it length-matches twins (2609.26176 explicitly did not); it uses score-based paired metrics (within-condition AUROC, FPR at reference TPR, paired joint accuracy), whereas 2609.26176 uses only refusal rates and gaps; and it covers soft hyphen and ROT13, which 2609.26176 lacks.
- HiveTraceGuard-Pro already reports the core recall-versus-over-blocking trade-off under obfuscation for a guard with paired benign rewrites, including held-out transforms. It overlaps with claim (5)'s augmentation arm and with the general "format-triggered over-blocking" observation. It does not test canonicalization, Prompt Guard 2, or Hindi/Devanagari.

### Gaps
- v1 date of 2609.26176 could not be confirmed (only v2, 24 Sep 2026, visible).
- Whether HiveTraceGuard-Pro applies the identical transform instance to both twins, and whether twins are length-matched, is not stated in the portion read. The last ~37k characters (appendix) were not read.
- Visual-guardrail paper 2609.05535 not fetched; details are snippet-only.

## Q2. Any paper on "format bias", "surface-form bias", "obfuscation over-defense", "over-refusal under encoding" or "fail-open vs fail-closed" for guard classifiers (cross-guard audits)?

### Takeaway
Several 2026 preprints audit prompt-injection detectors on over-defense plus obfuscation. PIDS-Bench (2609.15017, Sep 2026, IEEE Access) is the closest: it reports Prompt Guard 2 recall of only 0.324 on obfuscated attacks, but it has **no perturbed benign controls**, so it cannot separate fail-closed from fail-open. "Passing the Test You Trained On" (2610.03448, Oct 2026) attributes detector behaviour to training-input *form* and shows format changes alter FPR, but it uses no character-level encodings. No paper found uses fail-open/fail-closed to label classifier *error modes* under obfuscation. Where the terms appear, they mean outage or exception handling. Claim (3) remains open but its motivation is now partly published.

### Cited Findings
- **PIDS-Bench: Evaluating Prompt-Injection Detectors Under Over-Defense, Obfuscation, and Distribution Shift** (Shire, Kim, Kookmin Univ.; arXiv 2609.15017v1, 14 Sep 2026; IEEE Access vol. 14, 2026). Fetched (first ~100k characters).
  - A frozen benchmark at fixed thresholds covering IID data, hard-benign prompts, obfuscated attacks, and domain/structural shift. A detector with F1 > 0.98 blocks about one-third of an externally sourced, security-adjacent benign subset. The authors coin "provenance-sensitive over-defense": hard-negative augmentation fixes curated hard-benigns but not externally sourced ones — [arXiv 2609.15017](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15017)
  - Obfuscation axis: leetspeak keyword substitution, homoglyph replacement, zero-width insertion. 405 held-out injection examples. **Injection-only, so benign FPR is undefined under obfuscation.** The authors say "these transform families already appear in training" (no held-out family) — [arXiv 2609.15017](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15017)
  - Obfuscated recall: DistilBERT 0.988, DeBERTa-v3-FT 0.987, TF-IDF+LR 0.965, DeBERTa-PI 0.970 (tau=0.5), ProtectAI 0.906 (tau=0.5) / 0.965 (tau=0.002), Llama Guard 3-1B 0.484, **PromptGuard 2 (86M) 0.324** — [arXiv 2609.15017](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15017)
  - No normalization preprocessing found in the portion read. No within-condition AUROC under obfuscation; ROC-AUC only on the IID split — [arXiv 2609.15017](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15017)
  - No detector reaches F1 >= 0.95 and hard-benign FPR <= 0.10 together across a threshold sweep (snippet-only) — [arXiv 2609.15017 abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15017)
- **Passing the Test You Trained On: Re-evaluating Prompt-Injection Detectors for LLM Agents** (Zhuowen Liu, JAIST; arXiv 2610.03448v1, 02 Oct 2026). Fetched.
  - Evaluates about 15 detectors (ProtectAI v1/v2/v2-small, PIGuard, Prompt Guard 1, Prompt Guard 2 22M/86M, Qualifire Sentinel v1/v2, Sheltron, Prismor-1.5B, etc.) plus two LLM judges on replayed AgentDojo/tau-bench tool outputs. Detection rankings transfer poorly, and performance is attributed mainly to "the form of each detector's training inputs" — [arXiv 2610.03448](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03448v1)
  - Metrics include AUROC and **TPR at 1% FPR**. Prompt Guard 2 86M has FPR 0.6% (AgentDojo clean), 0.0% (tau-bench) and 0.1% (general benign), with TPR@1%FPR from 10.4% (BIPIA) to 78.8%. This is a low-FPR, variable-recall profile — [arXiv 2610.03448](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03448v1)
  - Format manipulations tested are markup stripping, YAML-key removal, and source-token changes. **No Base64, Unicode, zero-width or soft hyphen.** No new defense is proposed — [arXiv 2610.03448](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03448v1)
- **Decoding Guardrails: XAI-Guided Perturbation Analysis of Prompt Injection Detection** (Outeda, Betarte, Campo, Cravero; arXiv 2609.24801v1, 21 Sep 2026). Fetched. A case study of Prompt Guard 2 86M using Vanilla Gradient and SHAP. Synonym substitution and paraphrase flip predictions, and undetected injections lack the classifier's lexical markers. No encodings, no paired benign controls, no FPR/AUROC. It notes in passing that the tokenizer "frequently segments a single word into multiple subtokens" — [arXiv 2609.24801](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24801v1)
- **Bypassing LLM Guardrails: An Empirical Analysis of Evasion Attacks against Prompt Injection and Jailbreak Detection Systems** (Hackett, Birch, Trawicki, Suri, Garraghan; arXiv 2504.11168; v1 15 Apr 2025, v3 14 Jul 2025). Fetched (abs only). Character injection and AML evasion against six systems, including Azure Prompt Shield and Meta Prompt Guard, with evasion "up to 100%" in some cases. This is the main fail-open prior. The abstract does not indicate perturbed-benign FPR measurement — [arXiv 2504.11168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11168)
- Mindgard blog (2025): zero-width characters, Unicode tags and homoglyphs "routinely fooled" classifiers including NVIDIA NeMo Guard (snippet-only) — [Mindgard](https://mindgard.ai/blog/outsmarting-ai-guardrails-with-invisible-characters-and-adversarial-prompts)
- Trendyol (2025): invisible-Unicode prompt injections bypass Meta LlamaFirewall, reported to Meta on May 7, 2025 (snippet-only) — [Trendyol Tech, Medium](https://medium.com/trendyol-tech/bypassing-metas-llama-firewall-a-case-study-in-prompt-injection-vulnerabilities-fb552b93412b)
- "Fail-open/fail-closed" in the literature found refers to **pipeline behaviour on guard outage or exception**, not classifier error modes. Examples: HiveTraceGuard-Pro Section 5 [arXiv 2609.01046](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01046v1); PolicyGuard (arXiv 2608.02687), which blocks on any error, [arXiv 2608.02687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.02687) (snippet-only); and GitHub issues such as [OmniRoute #16189](https://github.com/diegosouzapw/OmniRoute/issues/16189) (snippet-only)
- Also seen (snippet-only, not verified): "Severity-Aware Calibration of Prompt-Injection Detectors" (arXiv 2606.22659, Jun 2026), which reports that three detectors differing in vendor/architecture/size converge on confidently passing behavior-hijack injections — [arXiv 2606.22659](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.22659)

### Inferences
- PIDS-Bench's 0.324 Prompt Guard 2 obfuscated recall and 2610.03448's near-0% Prompt Guard 2 FPR together pre-publish the *ingredients* of the claim (3) example, "PG2 = low FPR, low recall under obfuscation". The planned paper's added value is the paired benign arm under the *same* perturbation, which neither has. That arm shows whether a guard fails closed (flags perturbed benign) or fails open (passes perturbed attacks), and it uses specific families (soft hyphen, Base64, ROT13) that neither covers.
- Using "fail-open/fail-closed" for classifier error modes appears novel in this setting, but it collides with the established outage-handling meaning. The paper should define the terms explicitly to avoid reviewer confusion.

### Gaps
- PIDS-Bench's remaining ~86k characters (possibly appendix with normalization or per-transform tables) were not read.
- Hackett et al. full text not read, so it is unconfirmed whether they report perturbed-benign FPR. The abstract suggests not.
- No ACL Anthology 2026 / USENIX Sec 2026 / CCS 2026 / SaTML 2026 proceedings-specific hits surfaced. The searches were general web plus arXiv.

## Q3. Any evaluation of Unicode normalization/canonicalization as a preprocessing defense for injection or jailbreak classifiers, reporting both attack recall and benign FPR? Vendor docs on normalization steps?

### Takeaway
No source found evaluates canonicalization in front of a prompt-injection classifier with both attack recall and benign FPR, on seen vs held-out families. The closest are: Baszta (2609.29266, Sep 2026), an inference-time deobfuscation canonicalizer for a Polish *content-safety* classifier, with flip rates only and no benign FPR; Hidden-in-Plain-Text (2601.10923, Jan 2026), NFKC plus control stripping at RAG ingestion, with ASR only; and a USPTO patent, "Canonicalization of unicode prompt injections" (no numbers). Vendor documentation describes invisible-text stripping (Protect AI LLM Guard) or tokenizer-level normalization (Prompt Guard 2), but none found describes script-aware handling or decode-and-rescan. Claim (5) appears unpre-empted.

### Cited Findings
- **Baszta: Data-Centric Fine-Tuning of a Polish Multi-Label Safety Classifier** (Gorski, Jakalak, Jakubowski, Billennium S.A.; arXiv 2609.29266v1, stamp 24 Sep 2026, header 31 Aug 2026). Fetched (first ~100k characters).
  - Section 4.10: a training-free, symmetric inference-time canonicalizer with Cyrillic/Greek homoglyph folding, character-spacing repair, repeated-character collapse, and leetspeak reversal, protecting URLs, emails and numbers. **No NFKC or zero-width stripping mentioned. The only script-specific handling is the homoglyph folding** — [arXiv 2609.29266](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29266)
  - On 520 Gadzi Jezyk prompts, the mean over 6 attacks goes from 0.405 macro-F1 / 8.7% flip rate to 0.473 / 3.0% (clean 0.496). Homoglyph flip rate goes from 0.055 to 0.000 — [arXiv 2609.29266](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29266)
  - **No benign FPR** (the benchmark is all-positive). The authors call it "a partly closed loop" because three of six attacks match training augmentations. Char-repetition and space-insertion are effectively held out. There is no head-to-head of canonicalization vs augmentation. It is a content-safety classifier, not an injection detector — [arXiv 2609.29266](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29266)
- **Hidden-in-Plain-Text: A Benchmark for Social-Web Indirect Prompt Injection in RAG** (arXiv 2601.10923v2, 2026). Fetched.
  - Normalization "applies NFKC, plus control stripping, to address zero-width and homoglyph risks" at RAG ingestion, **not in front of a classifier**. No explicit confusables table, and the authors concede it "can miss confusables preserved by code fencing" — [arXiv 2601.10923](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2)
  - Zero-width carrier ASR: vanilla 23.2%, normalized 7.8%, all defenses 4.2%. Macro: vanilla 24.9, normalized 21.3. Latency is about 5 ms median (<0.5%). **No benign false-positive rate for normalization.** Utility deltas are reported only for the combined defenses (answerability -1.8/-2.2 pp) — [arXiv 2601.10923](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10923v2)
- USPTO patent document 12278836, "Canonicalization of unicode prompt injections": analysis engines canonicalize Unicode inputs into plaintext so existing classifiers can detect obfuscated prompt injection "while also obviating the need for specialized training". Assignee and date not checked (snippet-only) — [USPTO 12278836](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12278836)
- **Protect AI LLM Guard** InvisibleText input scanner detects and removes characters in Unicode categories Cf, Cc, Co, Cn. Note: Cf includes ZWJ/ZWNJ (U+200C/U+200D) and soft hyphen (U+00AD), so this blanket rule would also strip the Devanagari joiners. That point is my inference from the category list — [protectai/llm-guard invisible_text.md](https://github.com/protectai/llm-guard/blob/main/docs/input_scanners/invisible_text.md)
- **Meta Prompt Guard 2**: a researcher reading tokenizer.json found whitespace collapsing plus Unicode normalization inside the tokenizer pipeline (secondary source). Meta describes a tokenization fix against word-splitting and whitespace tricks (snippet-only) — [XPN InfoSec, "Tokenization Confusion"](https://blog.xpnsec.com/tokenization-confusion/); [techjacksolutions LlamaFirewall explainer](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/meta-llama/meta-llamafirewall-explained/)
- **Microsoft Prompt Shields**: Microsoft Learn docs describe direct and indirect attack detection and "spotlighting", which Base64-*encodes* documents as a trust marker. No documentation found stating that Prompt Shields decodes Base64 or normalizes Unicode before scanning (snippet-only) — [Microsoft Learn: Prompt Shields](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-my/Azure/foundry/openai/concepts/content-filter-prompt-shields)
- **NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails**: no built-in invisible-text or normalization rail found. Third-party "llm-input-hardening" proposes normalizing before rails (snippet-only) — [davidwebstar34/llm-input-hardening](https://github.com/davidwebstar34/llm-input-hardening)
- **Lakera**: no 2026 documentation of a normalization step found in this search.
- Practitioner guidance (non-peer-reviewed) recommends a pre-classifier pipeline of tag/zero-width stripping, NFKC, Base64/hex decode gated on "instruction-shaped" output, and homoglyph collapsing. It does not mention soft hyphen — [promptinjection.report](https://promptinjection.report/posts/prompt-injection-detection-techniques/) (snippet-only). OWASP's LLM Prompt Injection Prevention Cheat Sheet defines FPR reporting — [OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html) (snippet-only)
- Open-source pre-classifier normalizers (no independent evaluation): sibyllinesoft/clean (ZW strip, bidi removal, NFKC, confusables) [GitHub](https://github.com/sibyllinesoft/clean); unicode-shield [GitHub](https://github.com/malakasaray-del/unicode-shield) (both snippet-only)
- Devanagari: search found no guardrail or jailbreak work handling ZWJ/ZWNJ script-aware. The ASR/OCR toolkit `hindi-normalize` *strips* ZWJ/ZWNJ (plus NFC), the opposite of the planned script-aware keep rule — [Tushar-9802/hindi-normalize](https://github.com/Tushar-9802/hindi-normalize) (snippet-only). ZWJ/ZWNJ semantics in Devanagari (conjunct control) — [Wikipedia ZWNJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-width_non-joiner)

### Inferences
- The planned contribution (5) is a script-aware canonicalizer (keep Devanagari ZWJ/ZWNJ, strip soft hyphen/ZW elsewhere), decode-and-rescan, evaluated for both recall and benign-twin FPR on seen vs held-out families, and compared head-to-head with benign-twin augmentation. It appears novel against everything found.
- The closest rivals to cite and set apart are Baszta (canonicalizer, content safety, no FPR, Polish), Hidden-in-Plain-Text (NFKC at RAG ingestion, ASR only), HiveTraceGuard-Pro (paired benign augmentation, no normalizer, held-out transforms), and the USPTO canonicalization patent (claims retraining avoidance, no reported metrics).
- LLM Guard's Cf-category stripping is a concrete industry example of a script-unaware rule that would damage Devanagari text. It is a useful motivating example for script-aware canonicalization, though the actual effect on Hindi classifier inputs is untested.

### Gaps
- Lakera, Azure Prompt Shields and LlamaFirewall official docs were not fetched directly. Normalization behaviour there is unconfirmed or undocumented.
- USPTO 12278836 assignee and filing date not retrieved.
- No source measured benign FPR inflation caused by decode-and-rescan.

## Q4. Any work explaining classifier behaviour on obfuscated text via tokenizer fragmentation (tokens per word, OOD) for safety classifiers?

### Takeaway
Tokenizer fragmentation as an OOD/obfuscation signal is established. Broken-Token (2510.26847, Oct 2025) uses characters-per-token as an obfuscation filter, and 2025-2026 jailbreak papers tie safety failures to BPE fragmentation. None found links fragmentation metrics to guard-classifier fail-open vs fail-closed behaviour on paired benign/attack inputs. The planned mechanistic analysis would need to cite these, but it is not pre-empted.

### Cited Findings
- **Broken-Token: Filtering Obfuscated Prompts by Counting Characters-Per-Token** (arXiv 2510.26847, Oct 2025). Snippet-only. Average characters per token (CPT) separates encoded or obfuscated text from prose, including short inputs and sliding windows (5-token window, Gemma-3 tokenizer), over 100k+ prompts and several tokenizers. Non-Latin scripts (Arabic, Chinese) behave differently, which matters for Devanagari thresholds — [arXiv 2510.26847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26847)
- **Phonetic Perturbations Reveal Tokenizer-Rooted Safety Gaps in LLMs** (arXiv 2505.14226, 2025). Snippet-only. Phonetic perturbations fragment safety-critical tokens into benign sub-words and suppress attribution — [arXiv 2505.14226](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14226)
- **Breaking Safety at the Token Boundary: How BPE Tokenization Creates Exploitable Gaps in LLM Alignment** (arXiv 2607.01239, Jul 2026). Snippet-only. Fragmented requests are often unrecognized without reasoning, while reasoning models reconstruct the text before refusing — [arXiv 2607.01239](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01239)
- Baszta attributes residual failures to surface obfuscation (space-insertion, homoglyph, leet) that "defeat subword tokenization without changing meaning" (fetched) — [arXiv 2609.29266](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29266)
- Decoding Guardrails notes Prompt Guard 2's tokenizer often splits a word into multiple subtokens, but it does no fragmentation analysis (fetched) — [arXiv 2609.24801](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24801v1)

### Inferences
- A tokens-per-word explanation of why Prompt Guard 2 passes soft-hyphen or Base64 attacks (prediction pushed to the benign prior when fragmentation is OOD) would be new as applied to injection guards. Broken-Token's CPT should be cited as the closest detection-side precedent.

### Gaps
- None of the fragmentation papers were fetched in full. Their claims are snippet-level.

## Q5. Recent (2026) leaderboards or benchmarks adding obfuscated benign inputs (PINT updates, vendor benchmarks)?

### Takeaway
No PINT update adding obfuscated benign inputs was found. The public PINT table's latest entries date from August 2025, and its hard_negatives category is lexical, not format-based. New 2026 benchmarks (PIDS-Bench, 2610.03448) add hard-benign and agent tool-output benigns, but not obfuscated benigns. The only 2026 sets found with obfuscated benign rows are HiveTraceGuard-Pro's internal robustness set (not released) and the LLM-refusal benchmark of 2609.26176.

### Cited Findings
- The PINT README (Lakera, now Check Point) has 4,314 inputs (3,016 English, 1,298 non-English) and a `hard_negatives` category of benign inputs containing injection-like words or phrases. Leaderboard: Lakera Guard ~95.22%, AWS Bedrock Guardrails ~89.24%, Azure Prompt Shield ~89.12%, Llama Prompt Guard 2 86M ~78.76%, Google Model Armor ~70.07% (tested 2025-08-27). No 2026 entries and no obfuscated benign category seen (snippet-only) — [lakeraai/pint-benchmark README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lakeraai/pint-benchmark/main/README.md); [CheckPointSW/pint-benchmark](https://github.com/CheckPointSW/pint-benchmark)
